# 2026-10-20 — Where the Planner stands after S0 to S6c, and why the S-series is paused

A status note, written because the last three Planner iterations stopped paying and the picture was getting hard to hold.
All numbers, predictions and falsifiers are in `canopy-implementer/docs/prompt-and-plan-changelog.md`.

## What is solid

- The Implementer passes the hand-written plans of tickets and stock 10 of 10, order 7 to 9, shipping 3 to 5, loans 0 of 10
  (loans is an Implementer limit, not a plan one: the hand plans fail too).
- The Planner went from 1 of 50 hidden passes (S0) to 20 to 24 of 50 (S3, S4, S6) by moving everything computable out of the model:
  the steps from the API, the form of exception and BigDecimal wording, the checks on cases.
- Knowledge: `docs/principles/read-the-prompt-for-contradictions-first.md` (written today); the audit-versus-compensation line held
  again in the S5 checklist audit; "values and examples decide" (shipping 3 to 5 up to 10 of 10 with values for every case and a
  test sentence that did not contradict the rule).
- Tools: `score_planner.py`, `run_layered`, `run_step`, `analyze_runs`, the run-analyst; each batch is reproducible.

## What stalled

- S3 20, S4 24, S6 about 20 of 50 (tickets 5 plus 15 of 40 on the four older stories; S6c). Every fix moved one story up and
  another down. With n=10 and one Implementer run per plan, a difference of 3 is close to noise, and the Implementer's own
  run-to-run spread on identical plans was never measured (experiment A1: measured after this was written, see the end of this note).
- The S6a read (five failing tickets plans) found the failures in the plan's cases, not in the exceptions the S6 change targeted:
  a checklist that contradicts itself (`Optional.empty()` and `Optional.of(...)` for the same setup, plus a rule that every item
  must be covered, so the retry cannot repair it), `now()` in a case, a case that changes `issuedAt`.
- S6c: the `exceptions` field leaks the service's exceptions into units that throw nothing (a Rules line "no exceptions" above a
  Throws block of three). Whether that, noise or the harness commit caused 24 to 15 is not established.

## What it points to

The failures are facts that exist nowhere in the Planner's input, which the model then invents and the harness cannot tell are wrong.
For tickets: whether 90 minutes is inclusive, what `validate` preserves, which rejection wins, what persists. The best results also
rest on a hand-written `api.txt` (classes, signatures, exception types, responsibilities) that Canopy's own flow (`intent`, `spec`)
does not produce. So the Planner has only been scored on an input the product does not have.
See `docs/open-questions/planner-input-human-facts.md`.

## Course from here

1. Pause S-series tuning (no S7 audit, no more Throws changes) until A1 says whether S4 to S6c was real.
2. E1: the human-written example table for tickets (transition table, executable examples, decisions and non-goals), rendered by
   a script (A2), taken verbatim for cases and exceptions by the layered Planner (A1 arm), and with the gap rows removed (A3).
   Designed, not run. Predictions are in the open-question entry.
3. Open and unowned: loans at 0 of 10 with hand plans; the Implementer's repair step (a retry recovers rarely when the model's
   test and code disagree); whether a model can list the missing facts itself (E2).

## Addendum: A1 measured the noise (same day)

Re-running the stored plans of stock, shipping and loans through the current Implementer: the S4 plans scored 17 of 30 (originally 19), the S6c plans 14 of 30 (originally 10). The gap of 9 became 3, about one standard error.
Shipping reversed (S4 plans 5 to 2, S6c plans 2 to 5). So most of "S4 against S6c" was sampling, the `exceptions` field is not shown to hurt, and one Implementer run per plan with n=10 cannot separate changes of 3 on a story.
What survives: S0 (1) and S2 (7) against S3 to S6 (20 to 24) are outside the noise; the differences among S3, S4 and S6 are not. Future Planner comparisons need several Implementer runs per plan. The course in "What it points to" stands: the plateau is real,
and the lever is the missing facts, so E1 is the next experiment, with 3 Implementer runs per plan.
